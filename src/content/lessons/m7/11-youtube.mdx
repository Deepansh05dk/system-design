<Callout type="problem">
500 hours of video are uploaded to YouTube **every minute**. Viewers watch over a billion hours a day, on everything from a 5G phone to a smart TV to a 2G connection in a village. Video is by far the biggest consumer of internet bandwidth. How do you turn a 4 GB raw upload into something that starts playing within a second on any device, never buffers, and doesn't bankrupt you on bandwidth?
</Callout>

## Step 1: Requirements

**Functional**
1. Upload videos (up to several GB).
2. Watch videos on any device / network speed.
3. *(Supporting)* Video metadata (title, description), view counts, likes, comments, search, recommendations: mention as separate services.

**Non-functional**
- **Smooth playback:** fast start (< 1–2 s), minimal buffering, quality adapts to the network.
- **Global scale & low latency** for viewers everywhere.
- **Durable uploads:** never lose a creator's video.
- **Cost-efficient:** bandwidth and storage dominate the bill.
- Upload processing can take minutes (eventual availability is fine).

## Step 2: Estimation

- Uploads: 500 hours/minute ≈ 30,000 minutes of video per minute.
- 1 minute of video stored in ~6 renditions × ~50 MB average ≈ 300 MB → **~9 TB of new storage per minute** → over 12 PB/day. (These are YouTube-scale numbers; at interview time just show the method.)
- Viewing: 1B hours/day × ~1 GB/hour (mixed qualities) ≈ **1 EB/day** of egress. Only a massive **CDN** (often inside ISPs' networks) can serve this.

**Conclusion:** two separate problems: an **upload + processing pipeline** (heavy compute, async) and a **delivery** system (bandwidth, CDN, adaptive streaming).

## Step 3: Adaptive bitrate streaming

You don't send "one video file". Each video is encoded into several qualities (**renditions**: 240p … 4K) and cut into **segments** of 2–6 seconds. A **manifest** file (HLS `.m3u8` or DASH `.mpd`) lists all renditions and segment URLs. The player downloads segments one by one and **chooses the quality for each segment** based on measured bandwidth and buffer level.

<AbrSim />

Benefits: playback starts fast (begin at low quality), adapts as the network changes, seeking only fetches the needed segments, and segments are plain HTTP files: **perfect for CDN caching**.

## Deep dives

**The CDN is the system.** Netflix's Open Connect and YouTube's caches put servers **inside ISPs**. Popular content is pre-positioned during off-peak hours; long-tail videos are fetched from regional caches/origin on demand. The 80/20 rule is extreme in video: a small fraction of videos gets most views.

**Cost optimizations.** More efficient codecs (VP9, AV1 save 30–50% bandwidth vs H.264) for popular videos; skip high renditions for videos nobody watches; cheaper storage tiers for old, unpopular content.

**View counts.** Millions of views per minute on a hit video → sharded counters / stream aggregation, eventually consistent ("301 views" freeze in old YouTube was a deliberate verification step).

**Live streaming (follow-up).** Same segments + manifests, but produced in real time from the streamer's feed, with short segments (1–2 s, or low-latency HLS/CMAF chunks) and a few seconds of latency. Chat over WebSockets.

**Metadata and search.** Video metadata in a sharded DB (YouTube used Vitess on MySQL), search in Elasticsearch-style indexes, recommendations from offline ML pipelines.

<Callout type="frontend">
The browser side: `<video>` + **Media Source Extensions** via players like hls.js / Shaka / dash.js implement the ABR logic you just played with. Prefetching the first segments on hover, poster images and `preload="metadata"` make playback feel instant.
</Callout>

<Callout type="interview">
Separate the problem in two: **(1) upload pipeline**: resumable upload to object storage → queue → **parallel transcoding DAG** (chunks × renditions) → segments + manifests → ready; **(2) playback**: **adaptive bitrate (HLS/DASH)** segments served from a **CDN**, with async view analytics. Bandwidth is the cost driver; the CDN is the most important component.
</Callout>

<Takeaways>
- Encode each video into **multiple renditions**, split into **short segments**, described by a **manifest** (HLS/DASH).
- The player does **adaptive bitrate**: pick quality per segment from bandwidth and buffer.
- Upload: **resumable direct upload → orchestrated, parallel transcoding** (chunk × rendition).
- Delivery: **CDN everywhere** (even inside ISPs); efficient codecs for popular videos.
- Views/likes via **async analytics pipelines**.
</Takeaways>
