<Callout type="problem">
A Coldplay concert in Mumbai: 50,000 seats, 3 million fans waiting for 10:00 AM. At 10:00:00, everyone clicks at once. Each seat must be sold **exactly once**. People who pick a seat need a few minutes to pay. Bots try to grab everything. And the site must not crash. **This is the classic concurrency + traffic spike problem.**
</Callout>

## Step 1: Requirements

**Functional**
1. Browse events/shows and see a **seat map** with availability.
2. **Select seats and hold them** for a few minutes while paying.
3. Pay and receive a confirmed booking (ticket).
4. Unpaid holds expire and seats return to the pool.

**Non-functional**
- **Correctness above all:** no double booking (strong consistency for seat state).
- Handle **massive spikes** for popular events (100× normal).
- Seat map can be slightly stale (seconds); booking cannot.
- Fairness (first-come-first-served-ish) and bot protection.

## Step 2: Estimation

- Normal day: ~1M bookings/day ≈ 12/sec. Easy.
- Hot event: 3M users in the first minutes → **hundreds of thousands of requests/sec**, all fighting over **50K rows**.

**Conclusion:** average load is trivial; the design is about **(1) correctness under contention** and **(2) surviving the spike** without melting the database.

## Step 3: Data model

```sql
events   (event_id, name, venue_id, starts_at, ...)
seats    (event_id, seat_id, section, row, price,
          status  -- AVAILABLE | HELD | BOOKED
          hold_id, held_until, booking_id, version,
          PRIMARY KEY (event_id, seat_id))
holds    (hold_id, user_id, event_id, seat_ids[], expires_at, status)
bookings (booking_id, user_id, event_id, seat_ids[], payment_id, status, created_at)
```

The `seats` table is the **source of truth** for availability. It needs a database with **strong consistency and transactions** (PostgreSQL/MySQL, or a distributed SQL DB), sharded by `event_id` if needed: one event's seats live together, so a booking transaction stays on one shard.

Why a **hold** step? Payment takes minutes and can fail. Without holds, either two people pay for the same seat (then one gets a refund and is furious), or seats are locked forever by people who abandon checkout. Holds with **expiry** solve both.

## Step 5: Preventing double booking

<LockingSim />

For seat booking, the cleanest tools are the **atomic conditional update** (`WHERE status='AVAILABLE'`) and a **unique constraint** (one booked row per `(event_id, seat_id)`) as a safety net. Optimistic locking with a `version` column also works. Avoid long-held pessimistic locks during payment: that's what **holds** are for.

**Expiring holds:** a background job (or delayed queue message) releases holds whose `held_until` has passed: `UPDATE seats SET status='AVAILABLE', hold_id=NULL WHERE status='HELD' AND held_until < now()`. Reads also treat expired holds as available.

Other spike techniques: **pre-scale** before a known on-sale time, **pre-warm caches**, make the booking path minimal (no non-essential calls), and for general-admission (no seat selection) use a **counter in Redis** (`DECR`) as a fast gate before the DB, or process requests through a queue serially per event.

<Callout type="interview">
The two halves interviewers want: **(1) correctness:** hold seats with an **atomic conditional update** + **expiry**, confirm on payment with another conditional transition, **unique constraint** as a safety net, idempotent payments; **(2) the spike:** **virtual waiting room**, CDN/WAF/bot protection, cached seat maps, controlled admission rate. Mention that availability shown on the map is eventually consistent but the booking step is strongly consistent.
</Callout>

<Takeaways>
- **Hold → pay → confirm**, with holds that **expire** and return seats.
- Prevent double booking with **atomic conditional updates** (`WHERE status='AVAILABLE'`) + **unique constraints**; keep transactions short.
- Shard seats **by event** so a booking stays on one shard.
- Survive spikes with a **virtual waiting room**, CDN/WAF/bot defenses, cached seat maps and pre-scaling.
- Payments via provider with **idempotency keys** and **webhooks**; refund if a hold expired.
</Takeaways>
