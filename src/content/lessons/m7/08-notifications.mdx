<Callout type="problem">
Your e-commerce company needs to send "Your order has shipped" (push + email), OTP codes (SMS, within seconds!), and a weekly marketing email to 50 million users, through third-party providers like APNs, FCM, Twilio and SendGrid that are sometimes slow or down. Users must never get the same notification three times, must not get marketing at 3 AM, and an OTP must never be stuck behind 50 million marketing emails.
</Callout>

## Step 1: Requirements

**Functional**
1. Other services trigger notifications via an API or events: `notify(user, template, data, channels)`.
2. Channels: **push** (iOS/Android), **SMS**, **email**, **in-app**.
3. Respect **user preferences** (opt-outs per category/channel) and quiet hours.
4. **Templates** with personalization and localization.
5. Track status: sent, delivered, failed, opened *(stretch)*.

**Non-functional**
- **Reliable:** at-least-once delivery, no notification lost.
- **No spam:** deduplicate, and rate-limit per user.
- **Prioritized:** OTPs/security alerts in seconds; marketing can take hours.
- Scale: e.g. 10M transactional + 50M marketing notifications/day, with big bursts.
- Third-party providers are slow/flaky → isolate and retry.

## Deep dives

**Reliability.** Accept the request → persist it (or write to a durable queue like Kafka/SQS) → return 202. Workers ACK the queue message only after the provider accepts it. Messages that keep failing go to a **dead-letter queue** for inspection.

**Deduplication.** Producers may retry `notify()` and queues deliver at least once. Use an **idempotency key** per logical notification, stored in the notification log (or Redis with TTL); skip if already sent. Note: providers themselves don't always guarantee exactly-once, so "almost never a duplicate" is the realistic goal.

**Rate limiting & batching.**
- Per-user caps: "no more than 3 marketing pushes per day"; collapse bursts ("You have 12 new likes" instead of 12 pushes).
- Respect **provider rate limits** (e.g. SMS throughput per sender number): workers use token buckets per provider.
- Big campaigns are **throttled** and spread over hours so they don't starve transactional traffic or overload your own app when millions of users tap the push at once.

**Templates & localization.** Templates stored with versions per language; render with user data at send time. Marketing teams edit templates without engineering deploys.

**Scheduling & time zones.** "Send at 9 AM local time" → a scheduler stores delayed jobs and enqueues them at the right time per time zone.

**In-app notifications.** Store them in a per-user list (Cassandra/DynamoDB, partitioned by user_id) with read/unread state; deliver live over the app's WebSocket/SSE if connected.

<Callout type="why" title="Why separate queues per channel and priority?">
**Isolation.** If SendGrid slows down, the email queue grows, but push and SMS keep flowing. If marketing enqueues 50M messages, OTPs in the high-priority queue are unaffected. One shared queue would let the slowest channel or biggest campaign delay everything.
</Callout>

<Callout type="interview">
Cover: **producer-agnostic API/events**, **preferences + templates**, **separate queues per channel & priority**, **workers with retries/backoff + provider failover**, **idempotency keys + notification log for dedup**, **per-user rate limits/quiet hours**, and **DLQ + monitoring**. Mention that third-party providers are the least reliable part, so design around their failures.
</Callout>

<Takeaways>
- Services call a **notification API/events**; the system handles prefs, templates, channels.
- **Queues per channel and priority** isolate failures and keep OTPs fast.
- **Retries with backoff, circuit breakers, backup providers, DLQ** for flaky third parties.
- **Idempotency keys + log** prevent duplicates; **rate limits, batching, quiet hours** prevent spam.
</Takeaways>
