<Callout type="problem">
Your servers are in Virginia, USA. A user in Mumbai loads your homepage: HTML, 30 images, 5 JS files, fonts. Every file needs a round trip of ~220 ms across the planet, and the first ones also pay for TCP and TLS handshakes. The page takes 4 seconds. **You can't make light faster. What can you do?**
</Callout>

You bring the content closer to the user. A <Term k="cdn">CDN</Term> is a network of servers in hundreds of cities (<Term k="edge-location">edge locations</Term> or PoPs, "points of presence") that keep copies of your content near users.

## See the difference

<CdnSim />

Notice:
- The **first** request from a city is a **miss**: the edge fetches from your origin server, then caches it.
- Later requests from that city are **hits**, served from a few kilometres away.
- Your origin barely gets any traffic. The CDN absorbs most of it.

## Controlling what the CDN caches

The CDN obeys your HTTP headers (from the HTTP lesson):

```http
# Fingerprinted static assets: cache "forever"
Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable      → app.3f9a2c.js

# HTML: always check for a new version
Cache-Control: no-cache                                  → index.html

# API response shared by everyone: cache at the CDN for 60 s
Cache-Control: public, s-maxage=60, stale-while-revalidate=30
```

**Invalidation:** when you deploy, either (1) use **fingerprinted filenames** (`app.3f9a2c.js`), so new content = new URL, nothing to purge, or (2) send a **purge** request to the CDN for changed paths. Fingerprinting is the clean solution.

<Callout type="frontend">
This is exactly what Vite/Webpack do with hashed filenames, and why `index.html` must not be cached long. Your build tooling is already designed around CDNs. Vercel and Netlify are essentially a CDN + build system.
</Callout>

## What CDNs do beyond static files

- **Video streaming:** Netflix's Open Connect and YouTube's caches sit inside ISPs' networks.
- **Dynamic acceleration:** even uncacheable API calls get faster because the user's TLS connection ends at a nearby edge, and the edge has a warm, optimized connection back to the origin.
- **Security:** DDoS absorption, Web Application Firewall (WAF), bot protection. Attack traffic hits hundreds of edges instead of your servers.
- **Edge compute:** run small functions at the edge (Cloudflare Workers, Vercel Edge Functions) for redirects, A/B tests, auth checks, personalization.
- **Image optimization:** resize and convert to WebP/AVIF on the fly per device.

<Callout type="interview">
Any design with images, video or a global audience should have a CDN. Say: "Static assets and media are served through a **CDN** with long cache TTLs and fingerprinted URLs; the origin is S3. This removes most bandwidth from our servers and cuts latency for global users." For video designs (YouTube/Netflix), the CDN is the **most important** component.
</Callout>

<Takeaways>
- CDNs put copies of content **near users** to beat the speed of light, and **offload** your origin.
- **Pull** CDNs fetch on first miss (most common); **push** CDNs are pre-loaded.
- Control caching with **Cache-Control** headers; use **fingerprinted filenames** to avoid invalidation problems.
- Modern CDNs also provide **DDoS protection, TLS termination near users, edge compute and image optimization**.
</Takeaways>
