<Callout type="problem">
Your database cluster has 5 nodes and one leader. The leader crashes. The other four must pick a new leader. But messages can be delayed or lost, nodes might crash during the election, and **two nodes must never both believe they are the leader** (split brain → conflicting writes → corrupted data). How do machines that can't fully trust the network agree on anything?
</Callout>

## What consensus means

<Term k="consensus">Consensus</Term> is getting a group of nodes to **agree on a single value** such that:
- once decided, the decision **never changes**,
- the system keeps working as long as a **majority** of nodes are alive and can talk.

"The value" can be: who the leader is, the next entry in a replicated log, who holds a lock, the current configuration of a cluster.

## Majorities (quorums) are the key idea

With 5 nodes, a majority is 3. Any two majorities **always overlap** in at least one node. So two different leaders can never both win an election for the same term, because they'd each need 3 votes, and there aren't 6 votes to go around.

| Cluster size | Majority | Failures tolerated |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 2 | 1 |
| 5 | 3 | 2 |
| 7 | 4 | 3 |

That's why consensus clusters have an **odd number** of nodes: 4 nodes tolerate only 1 failure, same as 3, but cost more.

## Raft, step by step

<Term k="raft">Raft</Term> was designed to be understandable (unlike its famous predecessor, Paxos). Kill the leader and step through an election:

The core rules (simplified):
1. Time is divided into **terms** (1, 2, 3…). Each term has at most one leader.
2. The leader sends regular **heartbeats**. Followers that don't hear one within a **random timeout** (e.g. 150–300 ms) become **candidates**, increment the term, vote for themselves and request votes.
3. Each node votes **once per term**, and only for a candidate whose log is at least as up to date as its own.
4. A candidate with votes from a **majority** becomes leader.
5. Writes go to the leader, which replicates them; an entry is **committed** once a majority has it.
6. If a node sees a higher term, it steps down to follower. This is how an old, partitioned leader is safely demoted.

The random timeouts make it unlikely that two nodes start elections at the same moment; if they do (split vote), they just time out and try again.

## Where you'll meet consensus

You almost never implement Raft yourself. You **use** systems built on it:

- **etcd** (Raft): the brain of Kubernetes, storing cluster state.
- **ZooKeeper** (ZAB, a Paxos-like protocol): coordination for Kafka (older versions), HBase, Hadoop.
- **Consul** (Raft): service discovery and configuration.
- **Kafka KRaft**, **CockroachDB**, **TiDB**, **YugabyteDB** use Raft internally for replication.
- **Google Spanner / Chubby** use Paxos.

What they're used for in designs:
- **Leader election:** "only one scheduler instance should run jobs".
- **Distributed locks** with fencing tokens.
- **Configuration & service discovery:** a strongly consistent source of truth.
- **Membership:** which nodes are alive and part of the cluster.

<Callout type="why" title="Why not use consensus for everything?">
Every write needs a round trip to a majority, so throughput is limited and latency is higher (especially across regions). Consensus stores like etcd hold **small, critical** data (KBs to a few GBs of config, locks, leader info), not your user data. Use them as the **coordinator**, not the database.
</Callout>

<Callout type="interview">
When your design needs "exactly one" of something (one leader, one lock holder, one active scheduler), say: "We use **ZooKeeper/etcd** for **leader election**. It uses a consensus protocol (Raft) so a majority must agree, preventing split brain." You don't need to explain Raft fully unless asked, but knowing **majority, terms and heartbeats** lets you answer follow-ups.
</Callout>

<Takeaways>
- **Consensus** = nodes agree on one value, safely, despite failures.
- **Majorities** prevent split brain: two majorities always overlap. Use **odd** cluster sizes (3, 5, 7).
- **Raft:** terms, heartbeats, random election timeouts, one vote per term, commit on majority.
- Use **etcd / ZooKeeper / Consul** for leader election, locks and config, not as your main database.
</Takeaways>
